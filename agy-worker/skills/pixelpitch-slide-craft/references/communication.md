# Communication and narrative

A deck is a structured argument that happens to be visual. Design serves the
argument. These tactics decide whether it lands; the full canon is
[craft.md](craft.md). Apply it alongside [anti-slop.md](anti-slop.md), which
stays authoritative on bans.

## Spine

- State in one sentence what the whole deck proves. That is the spine.
- Structure descends by the pyramid principle: governing message → a few grouped
  supporting claims → evidence under each. Lead with the answer.
- Every slide is a load-bearing step toward the spine, or it is cut. If a slide
  leaves the audience knowing nothing new, merge or drop it.

## Direction (set before composing)
- Declare a one-line design read: deck kind, audience, tone, brand/style lean.
- Set three dials 1-10 and reason them from the read (baseline 7/4/6):
  VARIANCE (layout variety slide to slide), DENSITY (elements per slide),
  EMPHASIS (accent and scale contrast). Investor/launch leans high EMPHASIS;
  technical/data leans high DENSITY; editorial/calm leans low on both.
- Variance is composition, not system: distinct layouts per slide, but one type
  scale and ONE accent locked deck-wide — never a second accent for variety, and
  never one layout three slides deep.
- Copy tell: keep em/en-dashes out of titles; use the `…` glyph, not `...`.

## Assertion titles (highest leverage)

- The title is the takeaway written as a sentence, not a topic label.
  `Churn fell 40% after onboarding shipped`, never `Churn`.
- Headline test: a reader who reads only the titles, in order, gets the entire
  argument. If the titles read as a table of contents, they are labels — rewrite
  every one into a claim.
- One title, one claim, no "and". Specific and falsifiable.

## Per-slide discipline

- One idea per slide. A second idea is the next slide.
- Answer "so what?" on the slide — usually as the title or one emphatic line, not
  left implied beneath a chart.
- Concrete over abstract; every number carries a referent (baseline, comparison,
  or stake).

## Sequencing and signposting

- Order as setup → tension → resolution, not flat topic-topic-topic.
- Calibrate the evidence to the audience (engineer vs. CFO vs. board).
- Section dividers assert where the argument goes next; they are earned turns,
  not reflexive scaffolding. Do not number sections by default.
- One memorable thing per slide — a number, image, or phrase the audience repeats.
